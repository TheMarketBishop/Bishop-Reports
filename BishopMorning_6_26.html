03The Gamma Pin Yesterday 🔗

Yesterday's tape is the most important context for understanding today's setup. SPY closed +0.14% at $734.30 after gapping up roughly 0.7% on the MU print. What happened in between was textbook dealer mechanics.

32 green-to-red flips on SPY in a single session. Sixteen G→R crossings, sixteen R→G. A normal session has 5–10 crossings. Thirty-two is the algorithmic gamma-pin signature — dealer hedging at a strike so heavily loaded that price gets mechanically pulled back to it again and again. Market makers short calls/puts at $733 are forced to buy SPY as it falls and sell as it rises, pinning to the level. Result: zero net change, exhausted intraday traders, and pent-up directional energy that releases the next session — today.

The dark pool reversed structurally. Day two of the pattern break, and it accelerated. Yesterday's session: SPY −$2.95B (53 prints), AAPL −$977M (series record), IWM −$812M, QQQ −$536M, NVDA −$415M, AVGO −$403M, MSFT −$167M. MU was sold −$296M in the dark pool while the lit tape bought +$1.14B. Tapes disagree on MU. Net all-name dark pool flow: −$6.46B with ZERO buyers. First session in 16 weeks where every single name on the off-exchange tape sold. The 16-week SPY dark pool structural hedge that held through CPI, Iran, FOMC, and OPEX broke in two sessions.

The Mechanics Are Real, The Direction Has A Footnote
Quarter-end (Tuesday) and weekly OPEX (today) both amplify dark-pool selling. Pension funds, mutual funds, and index funds rebalance into Q2 close. Mechanical, not directional. But the pattern broke on a day MU printed +15% — institutions used the lit-tape rally to distribute in the dark pool. When the tapes disagree on a single name, the dark pool has historically been the better directional indicator. Worth noting, not worth panicking over.

The after-hours print to watch. 5:48 PM yesterday, after the session's record dark pool sell with zero buyers, GOOGL printed a $500M dark pool buy on NASDAQ Carteret. One print, after-hours. GOOGL moved higher AH. The dark pool that sold everything during the cash session bought GOOGL after the bell. This is positioning — not yet a confirmed pattern, but the first sign of where institutions want to be once the rebalancing window closes.

04GEX Structure 🔗

The dealer book is in deep negative gamma heading into weekly OPEX, with the engine reversing direction faster than at any point in the cycle.

GEX blanket: −$1,561M. Worsened from −$1,003M yesterday morning — the MU pop briefly improved the structure, but afternoon put-reloads erased it. The negative gamma profile is persistent. Zero near-term magnets above price. Nine of ten nearby levels are accelerators rather than dampeners.

Key levels: $730 sitting at −$182M (the major accelerator at price), $735 at −$145M above, $725 at −$145M below, $720 at −$141M further down. The downside chain is symmetric around current price — no directional pull, just amplification in both directions. Max pain: $731, currently at price. Expiration gravity and actual price have converged. The market is sitting exactly where the options math says it should.

The engine has reversed five times in four days. Monday AM positive → Monday PM positive → Tuesday AM flipped negative → Tuesday PM negative → Wednesday AM crossed positive → Wednesday PM reversed negative → Thursday AM positive → Thursday PM reversed negative. The structure can't hold a direction for more than 12 hours. Institutions reload puts every afternoon. The morning improvement evaporates by the close. Five reversals in four days is the oscillation regime at its most extreme.

The reload. Yesterday saw 4.93M new put positions opened, premium flipped put-heavy at −$63M (49% call), $1.23B of put premium. Institutions used MU's pre-market rally to refill the hedge book at higher strikes. The morning pop was the opportunity. The afternoon reload was the response.

The structural read. Weekly OPEX today clears 1.16M contracts (818K puts). Quarter-end Tuesday clears 1.67M contracts including 1.37M puts — the largest put-heavy expiration of the month. Two trading days of rebalancing remain after today. Wednesday July 1 the headwind expires and the July seasonality window opens. The oscillation regime has a known expiration date.

05Greek Forces 🔗

Vanna and charm flips are the next mechanical magnet. Wednesday's vanna loading was at +162.9K — the largest supportive position of the cycle. Yesterday's IV compression on the MU beat partially fired it; another VIX dip today releases more. With VIX at 19.58 and rising into the open, vanna is dormant for now but stays loaded.

Charm acceleration. Friday afternoons during OPEX weeks generate the strongest charm decay of the cycle. With max pain at $731 and price already there, the charm bias is for SPY to drift back to the pin into the close. Counter-pressure: heavy put open interest expiring forces dealer covering, which mechanically supports the tape on the close if puts go out worthless.

IV skew: −0.84% bullish. Fifth consecutive session of bullish skew. Despite five engine reversals, record bearish flow, and zero magnets — someone keeps paying for upside. The persistent call demand has not broken. This is the structural tell that the dip-buyers are still in the book even as the tape oscillates.

The Midterm Election Seasonality Frame
This is now the dominant macro frame. Historical pattern across 59 years: mid-term election years average roughly −4% trough into early October versus +4% for the typical year by mid-year. Chop or downside consolidation grinds through summer and Q3 — not a sell-off, just chop — and then both patterns rip hard into Q4, with mid-term years averaging ~+4% by year-end. The chop you're seeing right now is exactly that pattern playing out. Treat it as seasonal, not structural.

13Bottom Line 🔗

Bishop ReadStill bullish overall, but not breakout-ready. Asian markets are at ATH on leveraged trading, so after MU's print there's a second session of profit-taking. Profit-taking is not a top signal — it's consolidation, possibly consolidation to the downside.

The market is not yet risk-on. The chop between risk-on and risk-off behavior is primarily a function of mid-term election seasonality, which historically delivers chop and downside consolidation through Q3 — not a sell-off, just grinding. The pattern then ramps hard into Q4.

The more important question right now is where momentum is actually working. It's in Biotech (XBI, IBB), genomics (ARKG, TWST, TXG, PSNL, GH), healthcare (CVS, JNJ, HUM), and financials (JPM, BAC, C, WFC). Homebuilders are also picking up attention (XHB, ITB, TOL, PHM). Sector-wise: XLI, XLU, XLV, XLF.

Want to be risk-on. But will focus where momentum is until traditional risk-on names start breaking out. Once risk-on stocks start breaking out, that's when the book flips back to primarily risk-on. Until then, follow the tape.
