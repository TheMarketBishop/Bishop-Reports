Week in Review — What Happened While You Were Away🔗

The week away covered one of the most consequential stretches of the cycle. Here's the synthesis of what changed between the last report and this morning.

1. The market ran to a 7,501 all-time high — then OPEX gutted the structure. SPX blew through Alphatica's year-end target of 7,462 on May 13 and printed 7,501 on May 14, all in May. Then Friday's monthly OPEX drained 36.8% of SPY's gamma — the largest single-session rolloff ever tracked. Net GEX collapsed 54%, from the $1.64B all-time high to $753M. SPX closed Friday at 7,408, just 3 points above the new GEX flip at 7,405. The eight-week mechanical dip-buying engine lost more than half its fuel in one close.

2. Inflation re-accelerated and the bond market cracked. CPI came in hot, then PPI surged +1.4% MoM — the largest monthly increase since March 2022, far above the +0.5% expected. The 10Y yield pushed to 4.63%, the highest since February 2025 and above the level that triggered Trump's tariff pause in April 2025. Rate-cut odds collapsed to 2% for the year, with a 32% chance of an actual hike. Mortgage rates are nearing 7%. Rising yields are the macro headwind now sitting underneath the tape.

3. Kevin Warsh confirmed as Fed Chair; US-China trade deals signed. The Senate confirmed pro-crypto Kevin Warsh to replace Powell on May 13. Trump met Xi and announced a sweeping set of trade deals — China buying 200 Boeing aircraft, $17B/yr in US agriculture for three years, restored beef and poultry access, and the creation of US-China Boards of Trade. Xi visits the White House this fall. A genuine de-escalation on the trade front.

4. This morning: futures modestly higher, tech leading. US index futures are green — ES +0.13% (7,442), NQ +0.48% (29,372), Russell +0.29% (2,808). VIX is steady, with no panic bid in the volatility complex. Crude is soft (WTI −1.45%, Brent −1.35%) and Bitcoin is down ~2.2%, but the equity tape is holding constructively. Whales loaded millions in SPY and QQQ puts on Friday — the hedges are on — yet the market is not breaking on the thinner post-OPEX structure. So far it is absorbing it.

The Iran situation remains the live geopolitical risk: US and Israeli defense officials are preparing target banks for a possible resumption of combat operations against Iran as early as this week. Iran reopens its stock market Tuesday after an 80-day closure. Oil remains elevated versus the early-May $90 lows but is soft today (WTI −1.5%, Brent −1.4%). The Israel-Lebanon ceasefire was extended 45 days. The de-escalation that drove the early-May rally has stalled — the military track is active again, and it remains the key tail risk into the week.

The mechanics that mattered for eight weeks have changed. The gamma blanket that absorbed every headline with barely a flinch is now 54% lighter. The magnets above spot still pull — 7,450 (+$82M), 7,500 (+$105M), 7,600 (+$81M), with the center of gravity at 7,537, about 1.7% above spot. The bullish skew is intact. But the zone below the flip is the widest accelerator field of the series: everything from 7,000 to 7,275 is negative gamma, and the 7,300 strike is barely positive at +$5M.

What this means for the week. If the 7,405 flip holds, the magnets still function and the grind can continue with wider daily ranges. If it breaks, there's far less structure to catch the move — dips go deeper, rallies overshoot. This morning futures are holding modestly above the flip, which is constructive. But the cushion is thin (just 3 points at Friday's close) and the structure cuts both ways. This is the mechanical backdrop for Kevin's "retracement on the table" posture — the level is intact for now, but it would not take much of a downside catalyst to test it.

Dealer book: 59% smaller. The dealer delta engine dropped from +119M shares to +49M after OPEX. The mechanical dip-buying that caught every selloff for eight weeks is a fraction of its former size. This is the single most important structural change going into the week — and it's why Kevin is right to keep a retracement firmly on the table even while staying bullish on the bigger picture.

The Greek setup into NVDA week: vanna is neutral (VIX steady), charm is drained (post-OPEX), the dealer engine is 59% smaller. This doesn't break Kevin's structural thesis — it just means the index needs real buying, not dealer hedging, to hold. That's exactly why the focus shifts to individual strength: memory, AI, space.

Bottom Line🔗

Kevin called this stretch correctly — bullish all the way up through the run to 7,501, right on CSCO earnings, right on the retracements he expected last week. The structural thesis that carried the rally is intact: AI contributed 67% of Q1 GDP growth, memory names have doubled in a month, and the demand picture is confirmed at every layer. But the setup into this week is genuinely different from the effortless grind of April and early May. Friday's OPEX drained 36.8% of the gamma — the largest rolloff ever recorded — and Net GEX collapsed 54% from its all-time high. The dealer engine that mechanically bought every dip is down 59% of its firepower. Futures are modestly green this morning and the market is holding above the 7,405 GEX flip — constructive — but with the cushion thin and the dealer engine far smaller, Kevin's call to keep a retracement on the table this week is the right posture.

The most important part of Kevin's read: this is a market where you find the strength, not buy the index. With the gamma blanket thin and the index moving freely in both directions, passive exposure underperforms. Kevin sees the strength in memory (MU, SNDK), AI (GOOGL), and space/satellite names (NOK, SATS, SATL, SWKS). That's where the focus belongs this week.

Bull case (Kevin's structural view): The 7,405 flip holds, the magnets above (7,450, 7,500) still function, and the market consolidates calmly into NVDA Wednesday.
