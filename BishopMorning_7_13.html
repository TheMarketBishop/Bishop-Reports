00Glossary 🔗

Quick reference — key terms used in this report.

The BlanketNet dealer gamma — now +$1.11B, across the $1B threshold for the first time since the June crisis. This is the suppression regime that compressed daily ranges to 0.5% and carried the index from 7,200 to 7,600 in the spring. It's fully restored.
Suppression RegimeWhat a thick positive-gamma blanket does: dealers sell rips, buy dips, and compress volatility mechanically. Dips get bought within hours. Headlines get absorbed. It is the single most important structural fact on the board today.
Flip LevelWhere dealer gamma flips positive to negative — 7,448, now 127 points (1.7%) below spot. The widest cushion in the history of the series; the oscillation regime ran on 3–8 points. The regime boundary isn't near the edge anymore.
OPEX DrainWeekly expiration strips near-dated gamma. Friday's drain strengthened the structure (+$844M → +$1.11B) because it cleared the last near-dated puts while the longer-dated magnets above survived and grew.
Magnet CorridorThe band of call-heavy strikes that pull price. Every $25 increment from spot to 7,700 is now a magnet ($604M across 7,600–7,700), with no accelerators interrupting the path. Max magnet migrated 7,550 → 7,600.
Strait of HormuzThe oil chokepoint Iran declared closed this weekend. Roughly a fifth of global crude transits it. This is the one geopolitical headline that reaches past sentiment and into energy costs, inflation expectations, and Fed policy — hence today's counterpoint.
Max PainThe strike where the most options expire worthless — 750 on SPY today, drifting to 744 by Friday. It sits below spot all week, a mild downward tug against the upside gamma magnets.

02Overnight Developments 🔗

The weekend delivered a genuine escalation — Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz, the war resumed, and oil ripped. The market's answer, so far, is a shrug: futures opened down a tenth, and the S&P is off three-tenths pre-market. The structure that spent last week rebuilding is now the strongest of the series, and it is being asked to absorb the one headline it has never faced.

NEWHormuz is closed — and the war is back on. Iran declared the Strait closed "until stability is restored," with its Strait Authority calling transit "not possible." IRGC forces fired on commercial shipping; US aircraft downed an Iranian cruise missile and an attack drone; US forces launched further strikes, with explosions reported around Bandar Abbas and Qeshm Island — both on the Strait itself. Bahrain intercepted Iranian aerial attacks this morning. Traffic collapsed: roughly 6 vessels crossed in 24 hours against a ~25/day baseline. Iran's leadership adviser has ruled out any retreat, and the US–Iran understanding is described as being in a "crisis phase."

NEWTrump: the US is "taking over" the Strait. He framed it as guardianship — and said countries must pay the US for defending the passage. He also confirmed an 11-hour meeting with the Iranians and said the prior deal was broken. Brent surged 4.5% through $79; WTI added roughly 5%. Energy is the one asset repricing this — equities are not.

NEWSK Hynix landed — the biggest foreign listing in US history. Priced at $149, opened at $170, closed at $168.01 (+13%) on a $26.5B raise — past Alibaba's record — for a $1.27T market cap that makes it instantly one of the ten-or-so most valuable companies listed in the US. Demand was real: 7× oversubscribed, with roughly $5B to cornerstone holders. It moves to its permanent ticker today, and leveraged long/inverse products list alongside it.

NEWThe memory fundamentals got louder, not quieter. TSMC reported a 68% surge in June revenue — and reports Thursday. Chipmakers are now projected to generate roughly $430B in combined free cash flow over the next twelve months, triple the level of two years ago, while the big hyperscalers' combined free cash flow is projected to turn negative for the first time on record as AI capex balloons toward $1.4T by 2028. The picks-and-shovels are collecting the cash; the platforms are spending it.

CONTThe institutional bid never blinked. Last week's dark-pool tape ran +$24.2B net with the top ten conviction names hitting 10-for-10; NVDA alone drew +$7.34B and returned +8.3%. Friday set a fresh series record at +$3.29B. The blocks bought the sell, the bottom, and the bounce — and kept buying into the weekend risk.

03GEX Structure 🔗

The blanket crossed $1B. Net GEX hit +$1.11B on OPEX Friday — the threshold that defined the April–May suppression regime, the one that compressed daily ranges to half a percent, made every dip a buy within hours, and carried the index from 7,200 to 7,600. The round trip from the June crisis is complete.

OPEX strengthened the structure — it didn't drain it. The blanket went +$844M into expiration and +$1.11B out of it. The weekly drain cleared the last near-dated puts that were suppressing the reading, while the magnets above — sitting on longer-dated expirations — survived and grew. That's the pattern at every OPEX since May: the drain strips the negative gamma, the positive gamma persists, and the net result is a thicker blanket.

The cushion is now in a different zip code. The flip sits at 7,448 — 127 points, or 1.7%, below spot. That is the widest cushion the series has ever recorded. For scale: the oscillation regime ran on 3–8 points of cushion, and the June crisis ran with negative cushion (the flip sat above spot). Reaching the flip from here would take a major gap or several sessions of sustained selling. Eight consecutive positive-gamma sessions — the longest streak on record — sit underneath it.

No resistance to 7,700. All ten top strikes are magnets for a second straight session, and the max magnet migrated from 7,550 to 7,600 (+$148M). The corridor from 7,600 to 7,700 carries $604M and every $25 increment is a magnet — no accelerators interrupt the path. The center of gravity at 7,685 pulls 1.4% above spot. Implied vol rank sits at 13.5%, near the floor, even with a war resuming.

This Is The Regime That Absorbs Headlines
A $1.11B blanket with a 127-point cushion is the mechanical reason a closed Strait produced a −0.3% tape. Dealers suppress the dips, the magnets pull toward 7,600, and volatility compresses. The one honest caveat: this structure has absorbed military headlines all cycle — it has never been tested by a physical shipping closure, which reaches oil, inflation, and the Fed directly. That's what the flip at 7,448 is for.
04Greek Forces 🔗

Gamma — thick, positive, and suppressing. With the blanket back above $1B, dealers lean against moves in both directions. They sell strength and buy weakness mechanically, which pins price toward the magnets and dampens shocks. This is the single biggest reason the Hormuz headline is producing a shrug rather than a gap.

Charm — cleanly reset post-OPEX. Friday's expiration cleared the near-dated book, so time decay isn't pulling in any strong direction today. The magnets and the flow are in charge.

Vanna — a tailwind, with the caveat. IV rank at 13.5% sits near the floor of its range, so falling and low vol keeps dealers buying back into the move — the grind-up fuel is on. The flip side: vol this cheap means there is very little premium cushioning the book if the Strait closure actually starts repricing inflation. Cheap vol is a tailwind until it's a trapdoor.

What This Means For Today
The most constructive Greek backdrop of the entire series — thick positive gamma pinning toward 7,600, a 127-point cushion below, vanna working for the bulls. Lean the long side (memory/semis), and keep careful entries on: vol is priced for calm into a CPI print and a shipping closure, so size into strength and don't chase the extended names.

09Sector Rotation — Hardware Leads 🔗

Friday's tape was broad but the quality of the leadership slipped — materials and staples led, tech barely participated, and healthcare was the only red sector. It's the one wrinkle in an otherwise clean bullish picture: everything went up, but the defensives went up most.

Friday's close: XLB +1.25%, XLP +1.11% (staples), XLC +1.02%, XLU +0.62%, XLRE +0.50%, XLE +0.47%, XLI +0.45%, XLY +0.33%, XLF +0.31% — while XLK managed only +0.23% and XLV −0.82% was the lone loser. Staples and utilities outrunning tech on a green day is a defensive fingerprint, even inside a rally.

Underneath, the real rotation was within semis: money moved from memory (MU, MRVL, ALAB all red) into AI-compute (NVDA +4.0%, AMD +2.0%). The macro thesis is unchanged — hardware over software, picks-and-shovels over platforms, with chipmakers heading for $430B of free cash flow while the hyperscalers' turns negative. But the leadership inside the trade is shifting, and the SK Hynix listing gives memory money a new place to go.

The Trade Today
Lead with memory/semis — the conviction, the fundamentals (TSMC +68%, $430B FCF), and the institutional bid all still line up.
