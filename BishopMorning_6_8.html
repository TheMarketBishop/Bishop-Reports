The 0.864 P/C from Friday means an enormous amount of put protection is in the market — as that IV compresses, the hedges get unwound and the buying pressure is mechanical.

Charm builds this week toward the June 12 weekly expiry. No major charm impulse today — but the removal of the expired hedges is itself a tailwind.

Dealer book: massively reloaded on the selloff. The 2.58% drop forced dealers to add short delta at scale. The dealer book is now at its most loaded level since the rally began. When the bounce sticks and vol continues to compress, the forced buying from this short delta converts to the most powerful mechanical tailwind of the cycle.

This is the Greek setup Kevin's thesis has been building toward: maximum hedging (0.864 P/C) → maximum vol compression (VIX −4.7%) → maximum forced buying (dealer book reloaded). The trapped sellers + IV compression framework Kevin described is the mechanical explanation for why post-selloff bounces are violent.
