00Glossary 🔗

Quick reference — key terms used in this report.

GEXGamma exposure — net dealer gamma. Negative = dealers amplify moves; positive = they dampen them.
Gamma FlipThe price where dealer gamma crosses zero. Above it, moves get muted; below it, they accelerate.
Magnet / CorridorStrikes with heavy positive gamma that pin price toward them, dampening volatility.
AcceleratorA strike with heavy negative gamma that speeds price up as it is approached.
Window DressingQuarter-end cosmetic buying by funds to show core holdings in reports — mechanical, not directional.
Split TapeWhen lit-exchange flow and dark-pool flow disagree on direction — a sign of selective, name-by-name positioning.
Dark PoolOff-exchange institutional prints. Net buying/selling reveals positioning the tape hides.
JPM CollarA large quarterly options structure whose strikes can pin or cap the index; its expiry removes that anchor.
CharmDelta decay over time — how dealer hedges drift as expiry nears. Pins into events, releases after.
VannaHow delta shifts as volatility changes. Falling VIX pushes dealers to buy back hedges.
Midterm SeasonalityMidterm-election-year pattern — chop through Q3, strength into Q4; July historically strong.
ATM IVAt-the-money implied volatility — the market's near-term volatility expectation. Compression = calmer regime.

03GEX Structure 🔗

The oscillation that defined the last two weeks didn't ease — it reversed. The institutions that spent two weeks building the cage dismantled it in one afternoon.

Three shifts confirm the break. The flip cushion: 3 points from spot on June 18, 31 points above on June 26, and now 54 points below spot at 7,386 — the first time it sits meaningfully under price, not on it. The call volume: eight of the top ten volume strikes were call buying — +$208M of new call gamma loaded across seven strikes from 7,430 to 7,500, broad and sustained where prior windows were thin. The IV: ATM compressed from 16.8% to 14.9%, back to the May suppression-regime range.

Net GEX swung +$590M in a single session, −$325M → +$265M. The magnets at 7,450 (+$63M), 7,475 (+$58M), 7,500 (+$79M) and 7,525 (+$53M) form a $253M corridor above spot. Today's expected range is 7,400 – 7,525, with the 7,500 magnet pulling.

The downside infrastructure eased but didn't vanish: the 7,300 accelerator is now −$78M (was −$103M), 7,350 at −$64M, 7,200 at −$44M. If the put reload returns, the machinery to amplify is still there. But for the first time, the magnets above are stronger than the accelerators below — the ratio tilted. Structural floor: 7,386 near (−0.7%), 6,910 deep (−7.1%).

04Greek Forces 🔗

Gamma. The flip to positive net gamma changes the intraday character: dealers now buy dips and sell rips, dampening moves rather than amplifying them. Monday demonstrated it in reverse — +161.9M shares of forced dealer buying from call positioning and put unwinding overrode net equity selling. As Alphatica put it, the price followed the largest force, and that force was the options chain, not the order book.

Charm into the quarter-turn. Today is the final Q2 session, so dated positioning and window-dressing flows pin into the close. The release comes after — Q2-dated hedges roll off and charm bleed frees pinned exposure into the first sessions of July, lining up with the seasonal thesis.

Vanna. The IV compression (16.8% → 14.9%) is a vanna tailwind — as volatility falls, dealers mechanically buy back hedges, which is supportive so long as it holds. A re-expansion of IV on a failed Wednesday test would reverse that and is the cleanest early warning the bounce is rolling over.

Charm and vanna are theoretical frameworks for reading dealer behavior, not deterministic predictors — weigh them alongside price, not ahead of it.
