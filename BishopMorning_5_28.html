Overnight Developments🔗

The Memorial Day weekend produced the most significant geopolitical escalation since the Iran War began. Markets are returning to a dramatically different risk environment than where they left off Friday.

US struck Iran's Bandar Abbas. US F/A-18 jets hit a drone-control station at the port city on the Strait of Hormuz after Iran launched drones at commercial shipping. US CENTCOM confirmed intercepting Iranian drones and targeting the site. This is a direct US strike on Iranian sovereign territory — a significant escalation during what were supposed to be peace negotiations.

Iran retaliated by attacking a US airbase in Kuwait. IRGC fired a missile toward Kuwait, hitting or targeting a US military position. Kuwait condemned the attack. IRGC said "aggression will not go unanswered." This is a two-way kinetic exchange — the first since the early days of the conflict.

IRGC declared control of the Strait of Hormuz. Iran stopped 2 vessels, forced 2 more to turn back, and declared that obtaining IRGC permission is mandatory for passage. Only 26 vessels transited in 24 hours (roughly half of normal flow). This is a de facto partial blockade. 20% of world oil passes through Hormuz.

Israel struck Beirut separately. IDF confirmed a strike in Lebanon's capital overnight. A second theater escalating alongside the US-Iran exchange.

Oil surged, crypto crashed, equities remarkably flat. WTI jumped to $90.68 (+2.3%), Brent to $94.16 (+2.1%). Bitcoin crashed from ~$77,500 to below $73,000 ($500M in longs liquidated). But ES futures at 7,528 are only down −0.15%. The equity market is absorbing the worst geopolitical headlines since the war began with minimal reaction — confirming the structural floor underneath.

PCE in-line, GDP missed. April PCE: 3.8% YoY headline (est 3.8%), 3.3% YoY core (est 3.3%). M/M slightly below estimates. GDP revised down to +1.6% (vs +2.0% consensus). Claims at 215K (vs 213K est). The macro data paints a stagflation picture — growth slowing, inflation elevated — but nothing surprised to the upside enough to change the rate trajectory.

Global markets pulling back. Nikkei −0.8%, Hang Seng −1.3%, DAX −0.8%, FTSE −0.9%, Euro Stoxx −0.7%. Asia and Europe sold off on the Iran headlines. The US reaction so far is notably more resilient.

Kevin's framework: this is noise. The pullback is constructive. These are retracements from massive gains — SPY went from ~$700 to ~$750 in 8 weeks. Some retracement today and tomorrow is expected and healthy. Don't be heavy this week. Reload next week. The sell-offs-get-bought thesis remains intact until the structure breaks — and GEX at +$1.57B (ATH from Friday) doesn't break on a headline.
