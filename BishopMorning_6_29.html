00Glossary 🔗

Quick reference — key terms used in this report.

GEXGamma exposure — net dealer gamma. Negative = dealers amplify moves; positive = they dampen them.
Gamma FlipThe price where dealer gamma crosses zero. Above it, moves get muted; below it, they accelerate.
AcceleratorA strike with heavy negative gamma that speeds price up as it is approached.
CharmDelta decay over time — how dealer hedges drift as expiry nears. Pins into events, releases after.
VannaHow delta shifts as volatility changes. Falling VIX pushes dealers to buy back hedges.
Window DressingQuarter-end cosmetic buying by funds to show core holdings in reports — mechanical, not directional.
Net Call PremiumCall premium bought minus sold. Persistently negative = de-risking / call selling.
VXN–VIX GapNasdaq-100 vol minus broad-market vol. A wide gap means fear is concentrated in tech.
Midterm SeasonalityStatistical pattern in U.S. midterm-election years — chop through Q3, strength into Q4; July historically strong.
Dark PoolOff-exchange institutional prints. Net buying/selling reveals positioning the tape hides.
Implied MoveThe move options are pricing — the market's bet on a stock's reaction to an event.
OPEXOptions expiration. Clears near-dated gamma and can reset the dealer hedging regime.

Yet oil stayed pinned near $70. That is the whole thesis in one data point: the conflict generates headlines, not a durable risk premium.

NEWFed-adjacent: Hassett said the case for a rate hike "is not so strong" and flagged another strong jobs report — mildly constructive into Thursday's payrolls.

Friday's tape, for context: SPY closed −0.72%, and the session ended with a negative-gamma signature — a one-second cascade from $724 to $716.58 on the final tick, snapped back at the auction. The mechanics that produced it are still in place this week.

03GEX Structure 🔗

The OPEX did what the oscillation pattern predicts: it cleared the walls near spot. The question this week is whether the positive window extends.

Friday's expiration eased SPX gamma from −$452M to −$325M — improved, still net negative. The 7,400 accelerator collapsed from −$87M to −$3M as the near-dated puts institutions loaded there expired. The obstacle removed itself.

The flip keeps converging toward spot — 103 → 71 → 51 → 31 points over the last four sessions. It now sits at 7,385, 31 points above Friday's close. ES this morning near 7,460 has it trading above the flip: per the map, the regime flips positive and the magnets at 7,450 (+$17M) and 7,500 (+$33M) can pull with little interference.

The downside cage is still loaded, though. The 7,300 accelerator (−$103M) is the heaviest concentration 54 points below, with 7,200 (−$70M) and 7,100 (−$43M) beneath it — longer-dated puts that did not clear at OPEX. Structural floor sits at 6,910 (−6%).

One flag worth keeping: the 8,000 strike flipped to −$8M, its first negative reading, with put OI now above 567K. The hedging is extending further out and further down the calendar — the regime is not confined to near-spot weeklies anymore.

The Test
The first two post-OPEX windows lasted three sessions, then two, before institutions reloaded. If this window holds 4+ sessions, the oscillation is stabilizing. If it folds in 2 or fewer, the structure is deteriorating toward something more persistent. The short, thin holiday week is the stress test.
04Greek Forces 🔗

Gamma. The book is improving but not cleared. In a still-negative regime, dealer hedging amplifies moves rather than dampening them — Friday's one-second, 1% market-on-close cascade is the signature. In thin holiday liquidity that mechanic is more likely to show up during regular hours this week, in either direction.

Charm into quarter-end. As Tuesday's 6/30 close approaches, dated hedges and window-dressing flows pin and distort. The release comes after the quarter turns — 6/30-dated positioning rolls off and charm bleed can free pinned exposure into the first sessions of July. That timing lines up cleanly with the seasonal thesis.

Vanna. VIX easing to 18.68 (−2%) is a vanna tailwind — falling vol mechanically nudges dealers to buy back hedges, supportive if it holds. But the VXN–VIX gap sits at a 23-year-high 12 points: that tailwind is concentrated in the broad index, while tech-specific vol stays elevated. The mechanical support is not evenly distributed.

Charm and vanna are theoretical frameworks for reading dealer behavior, not deterministic predictors — weigh them alongside price, not ahead of it.

09Sector Setup 🔗

Friday's tape was a textbook defensive rotation — and it lines up exactly with the midterm-year playbook.

The leaders were defensives: XLV +3.0% (fresh 52-week high), XLRE +1.5%, XLP +0.9%, XLY +0.9%, XLU +0.8%, XLC +0.6%, XLF +0.2%. The laggards were the growth engine: XLK −1.9% and XLI −1.6%, with XLB and XLE −0.5%. Money left tech and industrials and went into healthcare, REITs, staples and utilities.

But look inside XLK: the index fell on its semis and AI-hardware weighting while software ripped — SNOW +9.7%, MSFT +5.7%, ADBE +4.8%, NET +4.7%, all green Friday. The rotation may be running within tech, not just out of it — hardware cooling, software turning. A theme on watch.

The fund-flow data confirms it — a record $9.3B out of tech funds, retail piling a record ~$12B into semiconductor ETFs as institutions distributed, and the VXN–VIX gap at a 23-year high. The crowd is long the trade the smart money is leaving.

The Seasonal Tailwind
In midterm-election years, healthcare is historically among the strongest sectors — the structural force behind XLV's leadership. The thesis stays anchored there. The one caveat: a fresh 52-week high is an extended entry, so the move is to wait for consolidation, not to chase the print.

11Bottom Line 🔗

Bishop ReadGeopolitics is noise, and the market just proved it again — strikes Friday, ceasefire Sunday, talks Tuesday, and futures higher through all of it. Only the truly major headlines deserve a seat at the table; this conflict no longer clears that bar.

The near-term call is chop. A four-day holiday week, quarter-end Tuesday, and thin liquidity that amplifies whatever moves do come — not the setup to force anything. The forward call is the patient one: July is historically the strongest month in a midterm-election year, and that is what we are positioning toward. June was bullish but narrow — Micron flew, most everything else consolidated. July is where the broadening should come.

Healthcare stays the primary focus — the strongest sector in the midterm playbook and the clear leader on Friday's tape. But XLV at a 52-week high is an extended entry, so the move is to wait for a pullback or consolidation to add — UNH, HUM, OSCR, CVS on the watch. Sizing stays light-to-moderate; full risk-on waits for traditional risk-on names to break out.

A newer thread worth tracking: software is showing the first signs of bullish momentum — SNOW, NET, ADBE, even Microsoft — green against Friday's red tape. Possibly the leading edge of a rotation within tech. On watch for confirmation, not yet a position.

Bull Case
ES is already above the 7,385 flip — hold it and the positive-gamma window opens toward 7,450/7,500. Retail Fear & Greed at 24.8 ("Extreme Fear") is a contrarian positive. Record tech outflows are rotating into the rest of the market, not out of it — that is broadening, and July seasonality plus healthcare leadership is the tailwind behind it.
Bear Case
The negative-gamma cage is still loaded below (7,300 at −$103M), thin holiday liquidity makes Friday's cascade mechanic more likely, and Tuesday's window-dressing unwind could expose the real signal — the clean Mon–Thu dark pool was distributing all week. The 23-year-high VXN–VIX gap says the AI complex may not be done correcting.
What Resolves It
Does SPX hold above 7,385 through Tuesday's quarter-end and the window-dressing unwind? Does the post-OPEX positive window last 4+ sessions (stabilizing) or fold in 2 (deteriorating)? Thursday's jobs print and whether semis / mega-cap tech break out are the tells for flipping from light to fuller sizing. Until then: patient, light, healthcare-focused, waiting for July.
